# Azalea Community Platform — Design & Launch Plan

## 1. Vision

Create a safe, joyful art community where people at partner facilities can share drawings and where visitors can respond by:

- Leaving encouraging, moderated comments
- Creating a respectful redraw
- Adding color when the original artist allows it
- Following the story of an artwork through its remixes

The experience can borrow the approachable feed structure of Tumblr or early Twitter, but it should not copy their engagement incentives. The platform should reward connection and creativity—not popularity, follower counts, or constant posting.

Use respectful language throughout the product. Refer to people as **artists**, **residents**, or **participants**, depending on each person’s preference, rather than defining them only by disability.

## 2. Product principles

### Consent before content

No artwork becomes public until the artist—or an authorized representative when appropriate—has agreed to the specific use. Public display, comments, redraws, coloring, attribution, and translation should each be separate consent choices.

### The original always remains intact

A visitor never edits the original file. A redraw or colored version becomes a linked remix with clear credit to the original artist.

### Caretakers contribute without becoming system administrators

Caretakers need powerful publishing tools, but not access to billing, security settings, other facilities, raw consent records, or unrestricted user management.

### Conversation without direct exposure

There should be no private messaging, public contact details, precise location data, or links that allow strangers to contact an artist directly.

### Meaning over popularity

Avoid public follower counts, rankings, streaks, rarity levels, downvotes, and “most popular artist” lists. A chronological or gently curated feed is safer and better aligned with the mission.

## 3. Recommended experience

### Public community feed

The main feed resembles a calm art journal:

- One artwork card per post
- Artist’s chosen display name or pseudonym
- Optional short caption provided with caretaker assistance
- Facility shown only at the approved level, such as “Partner artist in Kaohsiung”
- Traditional Chinese and English captions when reviewed translations are available
- Clearly marked buttons for **Send encouragement**, **Add color**, and **Create a redraw**
- A small remix count that opens the artwork’s creative family tree
- Chronological and tag-based browsing instead of algorithmic popularity ranking

Possible tags include nature, animals, places, imagination, celebrations, and coloring pages. Avoid diagnosis-based tags unless the artist specifically requests them and the facility approves.

### Artwork detail page

Each artwork receives a permanent page containing:

- High-quality, accessible image
- Alt text
- Artist attribution at the approved privacy level
- Caption and translation
- Original/remix status
- Allowed interactions
- Approved comments
- A visual “creative branches” section showing redraws and colored versions
- Report and content-removal controls

### Collaborative responses

Visitors can choose one of three response types:

1. **Encouragement:** a short comment submitted to moderation.
2. **Color this drawing:** opens line art in the existing browser drawing studio. The result is saved as a remix.
3. **Create a redraw:** provides a reference view and lets the visitor upload or draw a new interpretation.

Every response should include:

- Automatic credit to the original artist
- A note explaining respectful remixing
- Confirmation that the response will be reviewed
- Optional first name or pseudonym from the responder
- No public email address, social handle, school name, or location

### Caretaker updates

Caretaker updates live on a separate top-level public page linked directly from the main navigation. Caretakers receive a facility-scoped workspace for posting consent-approved activity notes, celebrations, new artwork announcements, and messages from participants.

- Updates use the same consent, translation, accessibility, and moderation process as artwork.
- A post can contain approved artwork or activity media, but never private medical or contact information.
- Caretakers can draft, schedule, submit, hide, and request removal of their facility’s updates.
- Every post identifies it as a caretaker update and records who submitted and approved it.
- Public readers can report an update, but cannot contact the caretaker or participant directly.

### Volunteer field notes and applications

Volunteers receive a separate top-level page linked directly from the main navigation for documenting previous fundraising events and service projects and applying to join the organization.

- Posts should state the event’s goal, activities, funds raised when verified, fund allocation, and lessons learned.
- Every photo or participant story requires appropriate permission.
- Financial claims require review against campaign records before publication.
- Volunteers can save drafts but cannot publish directly at launch.
- New applicants can submit an interest form describing their stage of education, interests, motivation, availability, language skills, and relevant experience.
- Applicants under 18 require parent or guardian approval before participating.
- Applications remain private and are visible only to authorized volunteer coordinators.

## 4. Community rules and enforcement feature

The public site should include a short **Community Promise** beside interactive content and a complete **Community Guidelines** page linked from every comment, remix, report, and submission flow.

### Rules shown to every contributor

1. **Lead with dignity.** Treat artists as peers. Do not use pitying, infantilizing, or diagnostic language.
2. **Protect privacy.** Do not request or post contact details, medical information, school details, or precise locations.
3. **Create with permission.** Only comment, color, redraw, download, or share when that option is enabled.
4. **Credit the artist.** Keep source attribution attached and never claim another person’s work.
5. **Keep participation noncommercial.** No sales, advertising, fundraising solicitations, NFTs, merchandise, or paid reuse.
6. **No harmful content.** Prohibit harassment, hate, sexual content, threats, exploitation, impersonation, and graphic violence.
7. **No private contact.** Do not ask participants, caretakers, or volunteers to move conversations off-platform.
8. **Respect moderation.** Do not evade blocks, repeatedly repost rejected content, or retaliate after a report.

### Rules interface

- Show a plain-language summary before the first contribution.
- Require an explicit agreement checkbox before submitting comments or remixes.
- Provide translated and accessible versions.
- Place a **Why was this moderated?** explanation beside rejected or edited submissions.
- Include a report button on every public post, comment, and remix.
- Offer report categories for privacy, consent, harassment, sexual content, hate, commercial misuse, self-harm concerns, and other safety issues.
- Provide a separate urgent-removal path for artists, representatives, and facility staff.

### Enforcement ladder

- Educational reminder for a first low-risk mistake
- Submission edit or rejection
- Temporary contribution restriction
- Account suspension
- Permanent account removal
- Immediate safeguarding escalation when someone may be at risk
- Legal or platform escalation for credible threats, exploitation, or unlawful material

Serious violations do not require a warning. Moderators should document the rule, decision, evidence reference, action, and appeal result without retaining unnecessary harmful content.

### Appeals and transparency

- Contributors can appeal once using a short form.
- A different moderator reviews the appeal when staffing allows.
- Artists and facility staff can always request removal without using the contributor appeal process.
- Publish aggregate moderation statistics, not names or identifying details.
- Review the rules with partner facilities at least twice per year.

## 5. Roles and access

### Visitor

- View approved public artwork
- Browse translations and remixes
- Start a response
- Report content

### Community contributor

- Sign in with a verified email or approved school/club account
- Submit comments, redraws, and colored versions
- View the status of their own submissions
- Delete their own unpublished submissions

Community contributors cannot contact artists, publish directly, or view private artist information.

### Caretaker contributor

This is the recommended “admin-adjacent” role.

- Create artist profiles using approved display information
- Upload or photograph artwork
- Add captions, alt text, tags, and artist preferences
- Record which interaction types are allowed
- Save drafts and submit posts for publication
- Review proposed translations
- View and respond to moderation questions about their facility’s posts
- Request removal or immediately hide a facility post in an urgent situation
- See approved community responses associated with their facility

Caretaker contributors cannot:

- Access another facility’s artists or drafts
- Change organization-wide settings
- View donor or payment information
- Promote another user to administrator
- Export the full user database
- Override missing or expired consent
- Permanently delete audit or moderation history

### Facility lead

- Invite, suspend, and manage caretaker contributors for their facility
- Confirm facility publishing rules
- Give final facility approval when required
- Review consent expiration notices
- Hide any content connected to their facility

### Azalea moderator

- Review posts, comments, redraws, and coloring submissions
- Approve, request edits, reject, or hide content
- Review reports and appeals
- Verify translations
- See only the consent status needed to make a publication decision

### Platform administrator

- Manage facilities and organization-level roles
- Configure safety rules and integrations
- Respond to security incidents
- Access audit logs

Keep this role limited to a very small number of trained adults or designated organization leaders. High school volunteers should not hold unrestricted production access by default.

## 6. Publishing workflow

### Step 1: Establish consent

Before upload, the facility records:

- Artist’s preferred display name or pseudonym
- Whether the artist wants the work posted publicly
- Whether comments are allowed
- Whether redraws are allowed
- Whether digital coloring is allowed
- Whether captions may be translated
- Whether the facility name may appear
- Consent provider and their authority
- Consent date, expiration date, and withdrawal instructions

The platform should store a consent reference and status. Highly sensitive signed documents should remain in restricted storage, not beside the public artwork.

### Step 2: Create a draft

The caretaker uploads the drawing and adds:

- Title
- Optional artist caption
- English and Traditional Chinese drafts
- Alt text
- Broad tags
- Interaction permissions
- Attribution choice

The system strips photo metadata, checks image type and size, scans uploads, and warns if a face, full name, address, phone number, or other private detail may be visible.

### Step 3: Facility check

The caretaker or facility lead confirms that the digital post matches the artist’s choices. The platform blocks submission if consent is missing, expired, or does not permit the selected interactions.

### Step 4: Azalea review

An Azalea moderator checks:

- Privacy and identifying information
- Respectful title, tags, and caption
- Translation quality
- Accessibility text
- Correct consent status
- Image quality and orientation

### Step 5: Publish

The post enters the chronological feed. The artist and facility can request hiding or removal at any time.

### Step 6: Moderate responses

All comments and remixes enter a review queue before appearing publicly. The caretaker can optionally preview approved responses, but should not have to moderate strangers alone.

## 7. Comment and remix safety

### Comments

Start with short comments, limited to approximately 280 characters. Block links, phone numbers, email addresses, social handles, and location requests.

Provide optional prompts:

- “I noticed…”
- “This made me feel…”
- “My favorite detail is…”
- “Thank you for sharing…”

Avoid comments that:

- Use pitying or infantilizing language
- Ask about a diagnosis or medical history
- Request direct contact
- Make promises or offer money directly
- Contain sexual, violent, hateful, or exploitative content
- Critique artistic skill unless constructive critique was explicitly requested

### Redraws and coloring

- The original artist chooses whether each interaction is enabled.
- Remixes can never replace or overwrite the original.
- Every remix links back to the source artwork.
- A contributor must describe what they changed.
- Remixes require moderation before publication.
- Do not allow remixing of a remix at launch; one generation keeps attribution and moderation understandable.
- Provide a one-click way for the original artist or facility to hide any remix attached to their work.
- Do not permit commercial use, downloads for resale, NFTs, or merchandise without a separate written agreement.

### Reactions

If reactions are added, use one supportive action such as **Send a bloom**. Show the artist that people responded, but consider hiding exact counts publicly to avoid competition.

## 8. Consent and safeguarding requirements

This plan needs review by the facility, a Taiwan-based privacy professional or qualified counsel, and someone familiar with California youth-organization requirements before launch.

Important requirements:

- Consent must be understandable and available in the participant’s preferred language and communication format.
- Capacity and legal-representative decisions must be handled by qualified facility staff, not website volunteers.
- Consent must be revocable, with a documented removal process.
- Public posting and remix permission must be separate choices.
- Do not publish medical details or diagnosis unless there is a clear, participant-led reason and explicit approval.
- Use age-neutral design and language; do not assume every artist is a child.
- Establish mandatory-reporting and escalation procedures with responsible adults.
- Keep a documented incident-response process for harassment, privacy exposure, or inappropriate uploads.
- Define how long unpublished, rejected, and removed content is retained.
- Provide a public safeguarding policy, privacy notice, terms of participation, and takedown contact.

## 9. Moderation model

### Launch model: everything is reviewed

Pre-moderate:

- Caretaker artwork posts
- Captions and translations
- Public comments
- Redraws
- Colored versions
- Profile changes

### Later trusted-publisher model

After a facility has completed training and a successful pilot, trained facility leads may publish low-risk artwork directly when:

- Consent is valid
- No personal details are detected
- The post uses an approved attribution format
- Comments/remixes remain separately moderated

Random audits and immediate rollback should remain available. Never allow direct publishing merely because someone has posted frequently.

### Review outcomes

- Approve
- Approve with a small edit and notify the submitter
- Request changes
- Reject with a clear reason
- Escalate for safeguarding or privacy review
- Temporarily hide while investigating

Every action should create an audit event.

## 11. Suggested technical architecture

### Frontend

- Continue with React and TypeScript
- Add routing for feed, artwork pages, workspaces, and review screens
- Reuse the existing drawing canvas for coloring and redraws
- Add accessible image descriptions, keyboard tools, and reduced-motion support

### Backend

Supabase is a practical first option:

- Postgres for users, roles, artwork, comments, remixes, consent status, and audit events
- Supabase Auth for verified accounts and staff invitations
- Private object storage for originals and consent files
- Public or signed storage for approved, metadata-stripped artwork derivatives
- Row-level security so caretakers can access only their own facility
- Server or Edge Functions for publication, image processing, and notifications

Other reasonable choices include Firebase or a custom backend, but role and consent rules must be enforced server-side. Hiding buttons in the interface is not security.

### Security controls

- Multi-factor authentication for caretaker, moderator, and administrator accounts
- Invitation-only staff accounts
- Facility-scoped permissions
- Short-lived signed upload links
- File type, size, and malware checks
- EXIF and location metadata removal
- Rate limiting and bot protection
- Append-only moderation and access logs
- Encrypted backups
- Session revocation when a staff member leaves
- Quarterly role review
- No production personal data in developer or test environments

## 12. Data model

The first implementation should include:

- **Facility:** partner name, region, settings, and active status
- **User:** account, language, verification, and global status
- **Facility membership:** user, facility, role, invitation, and suspension state
- **Artist profile:** facility-scoped display identity and attribution preferences
- **Consent record:** permissions, provider, dates, withdrawal state, and restricted document reference
- **Artwork:** creator, title, caption, translation, alt text, tags, permissions, and publication state
- **Artwork asset:** private original, safe public derivative, dimensions, and scan status
- **Comment:** artwork, author, text, moderation state, and translation
- **Remix:** source artwork, contributor, type, asset, description, and moderation state
- **Moderation event:** reviewer, decision, reason, timestamp, and before/after state
- **Report:** content, reporter, category, status, and resolution
- **Audit event:** staff action, affected record, time, and security context

Do not put consent files, diagnoses, or legal-representative details directly into public artwork records.

## 13. Game plan

### Phase 0 — Partner design and policy

Goal: agree on the rules before building.

- Interview facility leadership, caretakers, artists, and representatives where appropriate.
- Confirm preferred terminology and accessibility needs.
- Draft bilingual consent materials.
- Decide what attribution levels are acceptable.
- Define who handles urgent removals in Taiwan and the Bay Area.
- Establish moderation standards and response times.
- Obtain privacy and safeguarding review.

Exit criteria:

- Written partner agreement
- Approved consent flow
- Named safeguarding contacts
- Clear moderation handbook
- Confirmed technical owner

### Phase 1 — Private caretaker pilot

Goal: test the publishing workflow without a public social feed.

- Build invitation-only caretaker accounts.
- Add facility-scoped artist profiles.
- Build artwork upload, draft, consent check, and moderator review.
- Publish approved work to a private, link-only gallery.
- Add audit logs and urgent hiding.
- Pilot with approximately five to ten artworks and a small staff group.

Exit criteria:

- Caretakers can complete uploads without developer help.
- No user can access another facility’s private records.
- Consent blocks work correctly.
- Removal requests can be completed quickly.
- Facility signs off on the workflow.

### Phase 2 — Public art feed

Goal: safely open viewing to the community.

- Launch chronological feed and artwork pages.
- Add bilingual captions and accessibility text.
- Add safe sharing previews without exposing private information.
- Publish privacy, safety, and takedown pages.
- Add reporting and moderation dashboards.
- Run accessibility and security testing.

Exit criteria:

- All public posts have valid consent and alt text.
- Reporting and takedown procedures are tested.
- Staff can identify who published or changed every post.
- Public pages meet the agreed accessibility target.

### Phase 3 — Encouraging comments

Goal: introduce controlled community interaction.

- Require verified contributor accounts.
- Add short comments with prompts.
- Block links and personal contact information.
- Pre-moderate every comment.
- Add bilingual review where needed.
- Notify caretakers only after approval.

Exit criteria:

- Harmful-content and privacy test cases are blocked or escalated.
- Moderators can meet the promised review time.
- Artists and caretakers report that comments feel respectful.

### Phase 4 — Coloring and redraws

Goal: add collaborative creativity after the moderation system is proven.

- Add per-artwork remix permissions.
- Adapt the drawing studio for line-art coloring.
- Add redraw uploads and automatic attribution.
- Build the one-level creative branches view.
- Add remix review and artist/facility hide controls.
- Test image ownership and licensing language.

Exit criteria:

- Original files cannot be overwritten.
- Every remix retains source attribution.
- Disallowed artwork cannot be remixed.
- Artists and facilities can remove linked remixes.
- Moderators can compare source and response efficiently.

### Phase 5 — Trusted facilities and expansion

Goal: support additional facilities without weakening safety.

- Add facility onboarding and training.
- Introduce trusted publishing only for qualified facility leads.
- Add random audits and consent-expiration checks.
- Review moderation capacity before each expansion.
- Publish transparency reports with aggregate, non-identifying data.

Do not expand faster than the team can moderate.

## 14. Initial implementation order

1. Partner requirements and bilingual consent design
2. Authentication, staff invitations, and facility roles
3. Artist profiles and consent-status service
4. Secure image upload and metadata stripping
5. Draft and moderation workflow
6. Private pilot gallery
7. Public feed and artwork pages
8. Reporting, takedown, and audit tools
9. Verified community accounts and comments
10. Coloring and redraw tools
11. Creative branches view
12. Trusted-facility onboarding

## 15. Measures of success

Prioritize safety and meaningful exchange over conventional social-media growth.

- Artists who actively choose to participate
- Percentage of posts with complete, current consent
- Caretaker time required to publish a post
- Moderation turnaround time
- Percentage of comments/remixes approved
- Artist and caretaker satisfaction with responses
- Removal-request completion time
- Accessibility issues discovered and resolved
- Privacy or safeguarding incidents
- Repeat creative exchanges between communities

Avoid using likes, follower growth, posting frequency, or time-on-site as primary success measures.

## 16. Decisions needed before development

- What name should the community feed use?
- Are participating artists adults, minors, or both?
- Who can legally authorize public posting and remixing for each participant?
- Should caretakers publish directly after training, or should Azalea always provide final approval?
- Which languages need first-class support?
- Can visitors download original artwork, or only view it?
- Should approved comments be translated before the artist sees them?
- Who owns visitor redraws, and what noncommercial license is granted to display them?
- How quickly must urgent content be hidden?
- Which adult is ultimately responsible for safeguarding and incident response?
- Which organization or fiscal sponsor will own the platform accounts and data?

The safest default is to begin with a small, private, fully moderated pilot and introduce public interaction one capability at a time.
