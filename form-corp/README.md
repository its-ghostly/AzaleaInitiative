# Azalea Initiative — California nonprofit formation

Prepared October 31, 2026. Planning instructions and blank official forms; nothing has been filed or approved. Confirm current agency instructions and fees before submitting. Assumption: a standalone California charitable/educational nonprofit seeking federal 501(c)(3) public charity status. The name, mission, board, addresses, and eligibility remain to be confirmed.

## Start here

1. Agree on the mission, legal name, adult support, and board structure.
2. File California Articles of Incorporation for a **Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation**, using the 501(c)(3) version.
3. Organize the board, adopt bylaws and a conflict-of-interest policy, appoint officers, and record the decisions.
4. Obtain an EIN from the **IRS**, free of charge, after legal formation.
5. File SI-100 within 90 days of incorporation.
6. Apply for IRS recognition of exemption and California tax exemption.
7. Register with the California Attorney General within 30 days of first receiving charitable assets, unless exempt. This deadline can arise before IRS approval; do not wait for the determination letter.

Incorporation, EIN issuance, federal exemption, state exemption, and charity registration are separate processes. This checklist is not a claim that Azalea Initiative already has any of these statuses.

## 1. Prepare the incorporation filing

| Decision | Information to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| Legal name | Proposed name: Azalea Initiative, subject to availability and final selection. Search [California Business Search](https://bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov/search/business). A preliminary search does not guarantee acceptance. Name reservation is optional. |
| Purpose | Describe the actual charitable/educational programs and public beneficiaries. Do not submit a generic mission that does not match planned activities. |
| Street address | Physical corporate address, not a P.O. box or an “in care of” address. A separate mailing address may be used. |
| Agent for service of process | A consenting California resident with a physical California address, or a qualified registered corporate agent. The corporation cannot be its own agent. |
| Incorporator | Recommended: a parent/adult signs the filing. Being the incorporator does not confer ownership or prevent the daughter from being credited as founder. |
| Governance | Identify initial directors and intended officers; decide whether there will be statutory voting members. Volunteers and program participants need not be statutory members. |

Names and addresses in state filings are public. Keep SSNs, identity documents, signatures, bank details, and completed personal-information worksheets out of this public repository.

### File online — preferred

Go to [California bizfile Online](https://bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov/), create/sign into an account, and select the formation filing for a **California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation seeking 501(c)(3) status**. Portal labels may change. The corresponding paper form is [ARTS-PB-501(c)(3)](forms/ARTS-PB-501c3.pdf).

- Standard Articles filing fee: **$30**, excluding optional services.
- Complete the name, addresses, agent, and specific purpose.
- Choose the applicable public/charitable purpose option consistent with the mission.
- Retain the standard 501(c)(3), private-benefit, political-activity, and dissolution provisions. Do not casually rewrite them.
- Review the complete filing before signing and paying.
- Save the endorsed Articles and Secretary of State entity number in the organization's private records.

The PDF includes instructions and a paper-submission cover sheet. If filing on paper, follow its current mailing and signature instructions. Do not submit a duplicate paper application if you already filed online.

## 2. Set up governance, including youth participation

Recommended arrangement: daughter as founder and youth program leader; parent/adult as incorporator; a board with responsible adult participation; adult authorized officers for contracts and banking. This is a practical recommendation, not a statement that California requires every director to be 18.

California's nonprofit public benefit formation statute does not state a minimum incorporator age. California generally permits minor directors, subject to qualifications in the articles/bylaws. Minor contractual capacity and provider age requirements can complicate transactions, so do not assume a 16-year-old can independently open every account or sign every agreement. Have a California nonprofit professional review any arrangement relying on a minor as the sole legal decision-maker.

At the organizational meeting or through valid written action:

- Establish the initial board through the applicable incorporator/board procedure.
- Adopt bylaws specifying director selection, terms, vacancies, quorum, officers, and any voting-member rights.
- Adopt a conflict-of-interest policy and document initial disclosures.
- Elect/appoint the required officers: president or board chair, secretary, and treasurer or chief financial officer. Follow California's restrictions on combining offices; the secretary and treasurer/CFO may not also serve concurrently as president or board chair.
- Authorize EIN, tax-exemption, charity-registration and banking applications; select the fiscal year.
- Approve a realistic first-year budget and record minutes/resolutions.

Consider at least three directors with independent adult participation as a governance choice, rather than treating this as California's universal legal minimum. Review the interested-person rule in Corporations Code section 5227, especially before paying directors or their relatives.

No government form substitutes for bylaws or organizational minutes. They must be prepared for the actual governance arrangement; no completed bylaws are supplied here.

### Plan the eventual handoff

A charitable nonprofit has no private equity owners. Successors take leadership roles through the articles/bylaws rather than purchasing the founder's ownership. Include student-leadership transitions after graduation and board replacement procedures in the bylaws. Document elections/appointments, update signers and agency contacts, and transfer records securely. Donations remain the organization's charitable assets. A merger, dissolution, or substantial asset transfer is a separate legal transaction and may require Attorney General notice or approval.

## 3. Obtain the EIN

After legal formation, apply at the [official IRS EIN page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). The IRS application is free; no employees are necessary.

Included: [SS-4](forms/SS-4.pdf) and [instructions](forms/SS-4-instructions.pdf), for preparation or the applicable mail/fax route. Use the exact incorporated name. Follow IRS nonprofit entity-type instructions and identify the actual responsible party; do not name a parent merely as a nominee. Keep the responsible party's SSN/ITIN and the EIN confirmation in private records. An EIN does not establish tax exemption.

## 4. File the initial Statement of Information

File **SI-100 within 90 days** after incorporation through bizfile Online or the official [SI-100 PDF](forms/SI-100.pdf). Standard fee: **$20**. Provide the entity number, addresses, required officers, and agent. Repeat every two years in the applicable filing period and update required information when it changes. Calendar the deadlines yourself.

## 5. Apply for federal and California exemptions

### Federal — IRS

Use **Form 1023**, or **Form 1023-EZ only if eligible**. Both applications are submitted electronically through Pay.gov. The local [1023-EZ instructions](forms/1023-EZ-instructions.pdf) include the Eligibility Worksheet. Complete the entire worksheet; a small budget alone does not establish eligibility.

- [Form 1023-EZ official guidance](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023-ez)
- [Form 1023 official guidance](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023)
- [Pay.gov](https://www.pay.gov/)

The PDFs are preparation/reference documents, not a substitute for the required electronic application. Verify the current application fee at filing. Retain the submitted application, attachments, receipt, and determination letter. Do not represent the organization as IRS-approved before approval.

### California — Franchise Tax Board

Federal recognition does not automatically grant California exemption. For the usual eligible organization with an IRS determination letter, submit [FTB 3500A](forms/FTB-3500A.pdf) with that letter. If applying without a federal determination letter, or if another exclusion applies, review **FTB 3500** instead; it is an alternative application, not an additional universal filing.

- [FTB application routes and eligibility](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/)
- [FTB 3500A instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500A-instructions.html)
- [FTB 3500 alternative application and instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500-booklet.html)

## 6. Register with the California Attorney General

Unless an exemption applies, register within **30 days of first receiving charitable assets**, including contributions of value, not just cash. This is a separate clock from incorporation and IRS recognition. Use the [Registry's initial-registration page](https://oag.ca.gov/charities/initial-reg) for the online route, or follow [CT-1](forms/CT-1.pdf) and the [paper instructions](https://www.oag.ca.gov/charities/initial-reg-paper).

The CT-1 packet lists a **$50** initial fee; verify the live fee before payment. Prepare the endorsed Articles, current bylaws, activity description, directors/officers, EIN, and any IRS exemption application/determination already available. Disclose pending/not-yet-filed IRS status accurately rather than postponing registration.

## 7. Maintain the organization

Assign an adult compliance coordinator and keep a private calendar for IRS annual returns/notices (990-series as applicable), California FTB returns/notices, Attorney General renewal filings, and biennial SI-100. Filing obligations can arise while exemption is pending. Determine each deadline from the actual fiscal year and agency instructions; this folder contains startup forms, not every annual filing.

Keep approved governance records, accounting, donation restrictions, grant agreements, and agency receipts securely. Review activity-specific needs before running programs involving minors, events, food, transportation, paid staff, or public fundraising.
